# AWAL lexical research note: تشينة / لْتشينة

- Record key: `AWAL-LEX-TCHINA-001`
- Status: `documented_candidate` (lexical meaning and Moroccan form supported; exact local distribution and borrowing route remain open)
- Language: Moroccan Arabic (ary)
- Lemma(s): `لْتشينة` (reported Moroccan form); `تشينة` (variant reported by contributor)
- Latin forms: `lečīna`, `tšīna` (scholarly transcription; spellings vary)
- Meaning: sweet orange, `Citrus sinensis`
- Contrast: bitter orange, `Citrus aurantium`, historically associated with the older `nāranǧa` naming tradition

## Main scholarly evidence

Fajardo Rodríguez et al. (2013), “Fitónimos albacetenses, algo más que palabras”, *SABUCO*, no. 9, pp. 133–173, especially p. 149 (journal pagination; PDF page around 17 depending on viewer).

The article states that:
1. `nāranǧa` derives through Arabic from Persian `nârang` and Sanskrit `narangáh`.
2. The older referent was `Citrus aurantium`, not sweet orange `Citrus sinensis`.
3. Portuguese traders brought `Citrus sinensis` and `Citrus reticulata` from China's coasts to the West in the early 16th century and planted them in the Algarve and Portuguese possessions/colonies in northern Morocco, naming Tangier, Asilah (Arcila), and Larache.
4. The older orange name was reused in compound forms such as Portuguese `laranja-da-China` and Spanish `naranja de la China`.
5. The authors report Moroccan Arabic `lečīna` for `Citrus sinensis`, Algerian Arabic `čīna`, and compare American Spanish `china`.

## Open questions for future research

1. Locate older Moroccan dictionaries, vocabularies, travel accounts, and market records documenting `lečīna` / `tšīna`, with dates and exact locations.
2. Compare northern cities (Tangier, Tetouan, Chefchaouen, Larache, Asilah) with central, eastern, southern, and Saharan varieties.
3. Ask speakers whether they say `تشينة`, `لْتشينة`, `لتشين`, or another form; record singular/collective morphology, pronunciation, and plural.
4. Establish whether the immediate donor was Portuguese `laranja-da-China`, Spanish `naranja de China` / `china`, or a regional trade variety. Do not assert a direct Spanish route without evidence.
5. Check whether the form refers exclusively to `Citrus sinensis` or can cover oranges more broadly in actual speech.
6. Compare the term in Moroccan Arabic, Algerian Arabic, Tarifit, Tashelhit, and Judeo-Moroccan varieties; record each source independently.
7. Seek independent attestations from at least two speakers per locality before assigning a regional label.
